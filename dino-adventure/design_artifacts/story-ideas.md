# Story ideas

Ideas for choose-your-own dinosaur adventures. Each one fits 7–20 scenes per play and has room
for real branching. **The Runaway Egg** was picked first and is built in `../`. The others are
saved here to come back to.

The mockup page `round1-story-and-art-styles.html` has the same ideas plus the art-style mockups.

## 1. The Runaway Egg (built)

*Cozy adventure, ages 4–9.*

A rumble shakes Fern Valley and a big speckled egg rolls right up to Pip, a young
Parasaurolophus. Pip sets off to Smoke Mountain to find where it belongs. The twist at the end:
the rumbling was never the volcano. It was a very worried T. rex mom stomping around looking for
her egg.

**Why it works:** the goal is instantly clear (take the baby home), the egg wiggles more each
scene so time feels like it's passing, the T. rex is scary-then-sweet, and the hatching is a big
payoff.

### Scene map (as first planned)

The story branches and then meets up again: about 22 scenes written, 11–13 per play.

**Act 1: Fern Valley**

1. **The Runaway Egg.** Rumble, rumble, BONK! An egg rolls down the hill and stops at Pip's toes.
   *Scoop it up / Toot for help / Follow the trail uphill.*
2. **Grandpa Longneck.** The old Brachiosaurus can see for miles: "That egg came from Smoke
   Mountain. Who will go with you?" *Tops / Zippy / Shelly.*
3. **The Fork in the Path.** Two trails leave the valley. Your buddy knows a third.
   *River path / Forest path / Buddy's shortcut.*

**Act 2: The journey**

- River path
  - **Wobbly River.** Three "logs" float like stepping stones. They blink.
    *Hop the logs / Fallen-tree bridge / Ask nicely.*
  - **Lost Baby Stego.** A little Stegosaurus can't find its mom. *Help look / Keep going.*
- Forest path
  - **Whispering Forest.** Three raptors want the shiny egg. "Finders keepers?"
    *Share berries / Hide-and-seek / Stand tall and TOOT.*
  - **Glow-worm Cave.** Pitch dark, except for tiny lights. *Toot and listen / Follow the glow.*
- Buddy's shortcut (one scene, depends on the buddy)
  - **Tops: Bramble Wall.** Tops bashes a tunnel through the thorns.
  - **Zippy: Sky Scout.** Zippy flies ahead and spots the best way.
  - **Shelly: Old Tunnel.** A forgotten tunnel full of fossils and fun echoes.
- Every path meets up:
  - **Steamy Hot Springs.** The egg feels a bit cold. *Warm it in the steam / Wrap it in moss / Hurry on.*
  - **Firefly Camp.** Night. The egg starts to wiggle! *Hum a sleepy song / Tuck it in / Wake your buddy.*
  - **Giant Footprints.** Footprints bigger than Grandpa Longneck's. *Follow / Climb the lookout rock / Toot and listen.*

**Act 3: Smoke Mountain**

- **Mama Rexie.** The "volcano rumble" was her footsteps! *Hold up the egg / Hide behind a
  boulder / Toot the lullaby (secret: learned at camp) / Give the blue feather (secret: from the raptors).*
- **Crack, Crack!** The egg hatches. The baby sees Pip first and says, "Mama?"
  *Hug / Introduce its real mama / Help name it: Chomp, Peanut or Rumble.*
- **One of five endings.**

### Cast

| Who | What | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Pip | Young Parasaurolophus | The hero. Curious, a little nervous. Pip's hollow crest makes a big TOOT, and tooting keeps coming back as a choice. |
| The Egg | Big and speckled | Wobbles more every scene. Taps back when you're kind to it. Its warmth changes how it hatches. |
| Grandpa Longneck | Brachiosaurus | Slow-talking, sees everything from up high, gives you the quest. |
| Tops | Triceratops buddy | Brave and grumpy-funny. "If anything is scary, I'll bonk it." |
| Zippy | Pteranodon buddy | Fast and never stops talking. Flies ahead to scout. |
| Shelly | Ankylosaurus buddy | Slow, calm and wise. Knows old paths nobody else remembers. |
| The raptor trio | Raptors | Mischievous, not mean. Can end up as friends. |
| The Log Crocs | River crocodiles | Pretend to be logs so nobody bothers their nap. Polite dinos get a ride. |
| Mama Rexie | T. rex | Huge, loud and frantic with worry. Turns soft the second she sees the egg is safe. |

### What the story remembers

- **Brave, Kind and Clever stars.** Each choice adds one. The ending gives a badge for your biggest one.
- **Pip's pouch.** Sweet berries, a blue raptor feather, warm moss. Some later choices only appear if you're carrying the right thing.
- **Egg warmth.** Warm choices make a giggly baby. A chilly egg hatches with the sneezes (a funny ending, not a bad one).
- **Buddy memory.** Your buddy brings up what you did: "You asked a crocodile *nicely*? You're braver than me."
- **Friends made.** The Stego family, the raptors, the crocs, the glow worms. Friends can turn up at the end.

### Five happy endings

1. **Home Sweet Nest.** Mama Rexie carries Pip home on her head. The valley never rumbles again, because now she tiptoes.
2. **Best Friends Forever.** The baby comes to visit for playdates. (Mostly kind choices.)
3. **The Valley Parade.** Everyone you helped shows up to cheer. (Two or more friends made.)
4. **The Big Toot.** Pip and the baby sing the lullaby and Mama joins in with a ROAR. (Learned the lullaby at camp.)
5. **Achoo!** The chilly egg hatches with a sneeze so big it blows Pip's crest backwards. (Egg got cold.)

### How choices should feel

- **Short and pictured.** 2–3 choices, each a short action with a little picture so kids who can't read yet can still pick.
- **The characters answer first.** Every choice gets a quick reaction before the page turns.
- **No game over.** A "wrong" turn is a funny detour that loops back.
- **Things come back.** The berries, the feather and the lullaby all pay off later.
- **Secrets for replay.** Hidden choices plus a sticker page showing which endings you've found.

## 2. The Volcano Wakes Up

*Hero journey, a little more exciting.*

Smoke Mountain starts puffing. Pip has to warn the herds and lead everyone to Crystal Lake.
Choices are about who to help and which way to go, and the ending changes with who made it there
with you.

**Good if** you want more stakes and a "rescue everyone" feeling. Needs care so it stays fun
rather than scary.

Seeds to build on:

- Each herd you warn (Stegos, Triceratops, the Longnecks, the raptors) joins the line behind you, and the art shows the line getting longer.
- Choices trade speed against helping: stop to help the slow Ankylosaurus family, or hurry on.
- A rival who thinks the volcano is no big deal and can be won over.
- Endings: everyone reaches Crystal Lake for a big splash party; or the volcano just burps a big smoke ring and everyone laughs.

## 3. Who Ate the Moonberries?

*Mystery, ages 6–10.*

The valley's Moonberry bush has been picked clean overnight. You question suspects (sneaky
raptors, a hungry Ankylosaurus, a sleepy Brachiosaurus), collect clues and pick who you think
did it. The culprit is a surprise.

**Good for** older kids who like solving things. More reading per page, and branching mostly
comes in which clues you find.

Seeds to build on:

- Clues are things you can see in the picture (purple footprints, a dropped feather, crumbs up high), so a kid can spot them before the text says so.
- A clue notebook that fills up as you go.
- The surprise culprit: a family of tiny night-time mammals, or the Moonberries rolled away down a hill on their own.
- Wrong guesses get a funny "nope, here's my alibi" scene and send you back to look for more clues.

## 4. The Museum Time Slip

*Time travel, real dino facts.*

Your kid touches a glowing fossil at the museum and wakes up in the Cretaceous as a tiny
dinosaur. To get home they need three glowing stones, each guarded by a different dino. Fact
cards unlock as you meet each species.

**Good if** you want the player to be the hero (their name in the story) and sneak in some
learning.

Seeds to build on:

- Type your name at the start, and the story uses it.
- Each glowing stone is a small puzzle or kindness test with a different dinosaur.
- Fact cards ("A Triceratops had about 800 teeth!") collect into a museum shelf at the end.
- The ending: back at the museum, the fossil that started it all has a tiny footprint next to it that wasn't there before.
