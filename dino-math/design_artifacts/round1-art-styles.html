New game · art direction round 1 · 5 live mockups

Dino Defenders

A math blasting game for the family shelf. Meteors are falling on Dino Valley, and each one carries a number. Solve the problem, pick the right meteor, and your dino knocks it out of the sky. Six worlds take the math from kindergarten counting up to grade 5 fractions and decimals, following what the Common Core standards expect at each grade.

Each mockup below is a different art style, and each one also shows a different game mode at a different grade, so you can judge the look and the play together. Any look can run any mode.

Five looks

These are animated. Watch a full loop of each: a problem appears, it gets answered, and the game reacts.

A · Grade 1 · Meteor Shower

Saturday Cartoon

Thick outlines, a bright sky and a chunky orange rex. Three meteors fall with answers on them. Tap the right one and Rex blasts it with a fireball. It's the same family as the Endurance Docking cartoon look, so the shelf would feel consistent.

Feel

Answer
Tap one of three meteors
Kid fit
Easiest to read from the couch. Big numbers.
B · Kindergarten · Egg Count

Papercraft

Cut-paper hills and a paper sun on the same kraft paper as the game shelf home page. Eggs light up one at a time as they're counted, a ten-frame fills in the corner, and an egg hatches when the answer is right.

Feel
Cozy, crafty, gentle
Answer
Three big paper tags
Kid fit
Best for counting and picture problems. The calmest look.
C · Grade 3 · Sky Gates

Pixel Arcade

A 16-bit side-scroller at sunset. Steer a pterodactyl up or down through the cloud ring with the right answer. Picking a lane makes it a steering game, not only tapping, and it scrolls faster as the streak grows.

Feel
Retro, fast, a "real video game"
Answer
Arrow keys, or swipe into a lane
Kid fit
Older kids will love it. Lots of small detail.
D · Grade 4 · Fraction Feast

Storybook Watercolor

Soft painted washes on textured paper and a long-neck dino called Bronty. Word problems are told as little stories, and the pie is drawn as she eats, so the fraction is always visible. The wrong stones are the usual mistakes, like adding the bottoms.

Feel
Quiet, warm, bedtime book
Answer
Tap a painted stone
Kid fit
Lovely, but lower contrast. Best with read-aloud story problems.
E · Grade 5 · Volcano Cannon

Neon Jurassic

Glowing outlines on a midnight grid. A meteor heads for the valley while its timer ring runs down. Type the answer on the keypad and the rex fires a laser roar. Typing means no guessing from choices, which suits the harder grades.

Feel
Glowy, dramatic, big-kid cool
Answer
On-screen number pad, or the keyboard
Kid fit
Best for grades 4–5. Dark screen.

Made for a five-year-old who's ahead

These rules hold whichever look you pick.

Any grade, any time

Worlds aren't locked to age. He can play Grade 2 at five, and drop back to Hatchling Hill for a warm-up. Stars are kept per skill, so you can see what he's mastered.

Every problem is read aloud

The browser speaks each problem, and a speaker button replays it. A pre-reader can still play word problems and "which is more" questions.

Tap first, then type

K–2 use three big answer choices. From Grade 3 you can switch on the number pad, which is better practice because there's nothing to guess from.

Misses show a picture

A wrong answer draws the problem: dots, a ten-frame, an array or a pie. Then he gets another try. The first miss never costs an egg.

Calm or Blast

Calm mode has no timer. Blast mode speeds the meteors up as the streak grows, for the fluency goals in each grade.

It adapts inside a level

Five right in a row makes the numbers bigger. Two misses makes them smaller. The skill stays the same; only the size of the numbers changes.
