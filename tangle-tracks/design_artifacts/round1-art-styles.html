New game idea · art-style mockups, round 1

Tangle Tracks

A top-down maze made of railway. The train waits at one end, the station is somewhere in the tangle, and you flip the switches and turn the turntable until the route goes through. Every screen below is the same puzzle drawn six different ways, and every one of them plays.

To play: tap a switch or the turntable to change it, then press Go. You can still flip switches while the train is moving, except the one it is on. With a board selected, keys 1–5 flip pieces and Enter starts the train.

Stuck? Show the answer

Point the turntable's arrow right (east). Then set the switch just right of the station so it turns left into the platform. Two flips is the best you can do.

Ages 4–9 · Canvas 2D

Wooden Toy Box

A beech-wood railway on a felt play mat. The way a switch is set has painted arrows on it, and the way it isn't has a striped barrier, so children who can't read yet can still see where the train will go.

  • Big, soft tap targets and chunky pieces
  • Smoke puffs, a bump when it hits the buffers, confetti at the station
  • Joints and pegs between pieces, like the real toy

Ages 10+ and grown-ups · Canvas 2D

Signal Box

The track diagram from an old railway signal box. Every switch has a numbered lever in the frame underneath, and the turntable has a dial. White lamps show the route you've set. Red lamps follow the train, and its train number rides alongside.

  • Pull the levers or tap the diagram; both work
  • The route lamps are a built-in hint you can switch off for harder levels
  • Room later for timetables and more than one train

Ages 6+ · Canvas 2D

Transit Map

Drawn like a metro map: flat lines on white, named stops, a river and a park. The red line shows where the train will go and redraws the moment you flip anything, ending in a tick or a cross.

  • Works as an easy mode, or as a hint button in any other style
  • Very clear at phone size
  • Stop names give each level a little story

Ages 4–8 · rough.js

Sketchbook

Crayon on squared paper. The lines wobble a few times a second, like a hand-drawn cartoon. It looks as if a child drew the puzzle, which suggests an extra: a mode where they draw their own tangles for someone else to solve.

  • Red arrows for the way a switch is set, a scribbled cross for the way it isn't
  • BUMP! and YAY! written in, comic style
  • Wobble turns off for people who prefer reduced motion

Ages 8+ · Canvas 2D lighting

Night Lantern

It's night. Move your finger or mouse to carry a lantern over the tracks; the rest stays dark. Switch lamps glow green for the way that's set and red for the way that isn't, and the station's windows give you something to head for.

  • Turns the maze into exploring and remembering
  • The train's headlamp lights the line ahead as it goes
  • Fireflies, a moon in the pond, lit cottage windows

All ages · three.js (WebGL)

Toy Diorama

A small floating island in 3D, tipped toward you like a model railway on a table. Low-poly trees and cottages, yellow arrows hovering over each switch, real shadows, and a camera that drifts slowly.

  • Feels the most like a "real" game on a tablet
  • Easy to reskin into worlds: snow, desert, candy, a mine
  • Heaviest to build and to run on old devices

The pieces in this puzzle

Switch

One track in, two out. Tap to choose which way. A train coming in from either branch always rolls through.

Turntable

The train rolls on, the table spins it to face the arrow, and it rolls off that way. Even straight back where it came from.

Crossing

Two tracks cross. No choice here: the train always goes straight over.

Buffer stop

A dead end. The train bumps into it and you try again. There are two in this layout, plus the one behind the start.

Station

The goal. Get there with as few flips as you can: here, two is the best.

Ideas to build on

  • Stars for fewest flips. Each board already counts your flips; three stars for the best score.
  • Live mode for older kids. Switches can be flipped while the train runs (except the one under it). A faster train makes this a reflex game.
  • Route preview as a hint. The Transit Map's red line, or the Signal Box lamps, could be a hint button in any style.
  • More to do on the way. Passengers to pick up, two trains that mustn't meet, colour-coded cargo for matching stations.
  • Draw your own. A level editor in the Sketchbook look, so kids can set puzzles for each other.
